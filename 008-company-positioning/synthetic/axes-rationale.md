# Competitive Quadrant — Axes Rationale (Fictional: Project Management Tools)

## Quadrant 1: AI Claim Credibility vs. Proof Point Density

### X-axis: AI Feature Maturity
- **Low:** AI is marketing narrative only; no shipped features with verifiable outputs
- **High:** AI features are shipped, measurable, and backed by customer accuracy data

### Y-axis: Proof Point Density
- **Low:** Social proof is thin — testimonials only, no case studies, no benchmarks
- **High:** Dense proof — ROI calculators, published case studies, G2 reviews, analyst recognition

**Why this view:** This view separates companies that are shipping real AI value from those inflating AI language for positioning purposes, while also revealing which companies have built the customer evidence infrastructure to support their claims at enterprise scale.

### Company Placements

**Stackly** (x=2.5, y=2.0)
AI summaries are shipped but trust is eroding from accuracy complaints. No published benchmarks, no cited accuracy rates, no customer validation of AI output quality. Proof point density is low — a handful of G2 reviews and one logo wall.

**Asana** (x=6.0, y=8.0)
AI Studio and AI Teammates are live with use-case specificity. Extensive G2 review base, Fortune 100 customer stories, and published ROI frameworks support the claims. The Work Graph narrative is backed by customer evidence.

**Monday** (x=5.5, y=6.5)
mondayDB 3.0 and AI agents are shipped but accuracy and governance claims are thin. Strong brand recognition and review volume but limited enterprise-grade proof for the autonomous AI narrative.

**Linear** (x=3.0, y=4.0)
AI features are lightweight (cycle time suggestions, PR summaries) and not a primary positioning pillar. Proof points are developer-community driven — strong in quality, narrow in enterprise breadth.

**Notion** (x=4.0, y=5.5)
Custom Agents are operationally deployed via FDEs but accuracy and governance are unaddressed publicly. Strong G2 presence and 100M user social proof, but enterprise AI validation is thin.

## Quadrant 2: Persona Specificity vs. Integration Depth

### X-axis: Target Persona Clarity
- **Low:** Horizontal tool for "all teams" — no dominant persona ownership
- **High:** Dominant ownership of a specific buyer persona (ops lead, engineering team, finance PM)

### Y-axis: Integration Ecosystem Depth
- **Low:** Few native integrations; relies on middleware (Zapier, Make) for connectivity
- **High:** Deep native integrations with category-dominant tools (JIRA, Salesforce, GitHub, Slack)

**Why this view:** This view reveals which companies have made a deliberate persona bet vs. remaining horizontal — a positioning clarity dimension — while simultaneously exposing which companies have the integration depth to survive procurement scrutiny at mid-market and enterprise scale.

### Company Placements

**Stackly** (x=7.0, y=2.5)
Strong ops-team persona ownership with explicit "non-technical project lead" positioning. But integration infrastructure is weak — JIRA/GitHub sync runs through Zapier and breaks regularly. High persona clarity, low integration depth: a ceiling in mid-market deals.

**Asana** (x=4.0, y=7.5)
Cross-functional positioning for "all work" — strong horizontal presence but no dominant persona ownership. Integration ecosystem is mature (200+ native connectors, Salesforce bidirectional sync, JIRA two-way).

**Monday** (x=4.5, y=7.0)
Marketing, Operations, and CRM personas are claimed simultaneously — breadth over specificity. Integration marketplace is large and native depth is strong across enterprise categories.

**Linear** (x=9.0, y=5.0)
Owns the engineering team persona almost exclusively — a strength in ICP clarity that becomes a ceiling for cross-functional expansion. Native GitHub/GitLab integrations are best-in-class but non-engineering connectivity is thin.

**Notion** (x=3.0, y=4.5)
Horizontal workspace for "all knowledge work" — persona is deliberately undefined to preserve breadth. Integration ecosystem is growing (MCP-based agentic connectors) but lacks the native depth of workflow-specific tools.
