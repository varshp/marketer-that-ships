**Key observations:**

- **Engineering-heavy hiring signals product-led growth phase.** At 35% of headcount, Stackly is still building before scaling GTM. The sales roles (8) are a recent addition — job postings reference "our first enterprise AE" and "founding SDR team," suggesting this is the beginning of a sales motion, not a mature one.
- **Four PM roles for a ~150 person company is unusually dense.** Three of the four are focused on specific product areas: integrations, AI, and enterprise features. This signals known product gaps being staffed to close.
- **Marketing is thin and tactical.** The three marketing roles are all demand gen and content — no brand, no PMM, no competitive. Stackly is not yet investing in positioning infrastructure.
- **Customer Success skews toward onboarding.** Three of five CS roles mention "onboarding" or "implementation" in the title, confirming that time-to-value friction is an operational priority.

### Inferred strategic priorities

**1. Fix the AI accuracy problem before it becomes a category narrative**

The two PM roles mentioning "AI confidence scoring" and one engineering role titled "AI Reliability Engineer" signal that Stackly knows its AI summaries are a trust liability. They are building a confidence layer — likely showing users when the AI is certain vs. uncertain — before this becomes a competitor talking point.

**2. Close the integration gap with a native engineering layer**

The dedicated Integrations PM role and two backend engineers specifically tagged to JIRA/GitHub/Slack connectors confirm that the Zapier bridge is being deprecated. Expect native integrations in H2 2026. Competitors have a 6-month window to exploit this gap.

**3. Move upmarket with enterprise readiness investments**

SOC 2 preparation, granular RBAC, and audit logs are explicitly on the 2026 roadmap per the Enterprise PM posting. This is not yet shipped — but the intent is clear. Stackly wants to move from team-level to department-level deals, and then to company-wide procurement.

**4. Make budget intelligence a category-defining differentiator**

Budget-to-task linking is Stackly's most unique feature and it is doubling down. The roadmap includes ERP integrations (NetSuite, QuickBooks) and AI-generated budget forecasts. If they execute, "project management that knows what things cost" could be a durable positioning wedge no competitor currently owns.

---

### PMM actions

**1. Attack the AI accuracy gap now — before confidence scoring ships.**
Stackly is internally aware that its AI summaries are unreliable and is building a fix. You have a 6-month window before the fix lands. Create content and sales materials that make "AI you can trust" a category expectation — and establish your product as the benchmark before Stackly's confidence scoring makes the comparison harder.

**2. Lock in enterprise accounts before their SOC 2 lands.**
Stackly's enterprise readiness features (SOC 2, RBAC, audit logs) are on the 2026 roadmap but not yet shipped. Any prospect currently in a security review where Stackly is being evaluated can be won today on compliance grounds. Build a "current state vs. roadmap promises" battle card.

**3. Don't let them own "budget intelligence."**
If you have any budget tracking, resource planning, or cost forecasting capability, start claiming "project financial intelligence" language now — before Stackly's ERP integrations and AI budget forecasts make it their exclusive territory. The category term is up for grabs.
