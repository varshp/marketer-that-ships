## Competitor Page Decoder: stackly.io

### Their core pitch
Stackly positions itself as the project management platform built for ops-heavy mid-market teams who have outgrown spreadsheets and find enterprise PM tools too complex and expensive. Their headline: "One place to run every project. No consultants required."

### Who they're targeting
- **Primary buyer:** Operations Managers and Heads of PMO at companies with 50–500 employees who own cross-functional projects and are tired of stitching together Asana, Confluence, and Google Sheets.
- **Secondary buyer:** VP Engineering / Director of Product at SaaS companies who need better visibility into delivery without adding PM headcount.
- **Pain points signaled:** Too many tools, status updates that live in Slack and email instead of a system, projects that fall off track without anyone noticing until it's too late, and executive reporting that takes hours to assemble manually.

### Top 3 positioning bets
1. **"Visibility without meetings."** The core promise is that Stackly replaces the weekly status meeting with a live dashboard — targeting the recurring frustration that PMs spend more time reporting on work than doing it.
2. **"PM software that ops teams can actually use."** They explicitly distance from the "enterprise PM tools" category (Smartsheet, Wrike, Planview) by emphasizing speed of setup and no professional services required.
3. **"AI that works in the background, not in your face."** Their AI feature framing is about eliminating manual status roll-ups — not a chatbot, but an automated summarizer that keeps leadership informed.

### What they're not saying
- **No mention of data export or portability.** The absence of any "your data" language suggests customers who care about data ownership should probe this.
- **Integration depth is thin.** The integrations page lists 12 tools via Zapier. For companies with complex tool stacks, this is a fragility risk they're not addressing.
- **No enterprise security language.** No mention of SSO, SCIM, audit logs, or compliance certifications — this is a firm ceiling on deals above 200 seats.

### PMM actions
1. **Build a "Stackly vs. us" comparison page** targeting the "ops team outgrew spreadsheets" moment — that's the wedge they're winning on.
2. **Lead with integration depth** as a differentiator — their Zapier dependency is a known pain point in reviews and an easy wedge for companies with JIRA or GitHub.
3. **Create an enterprise-readiness checklist** (SSO, RBAC, audit logs, SLA) that Stackly cannot check — useful at late-stage deals.
