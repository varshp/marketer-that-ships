# Why Your PM Tool Is Why You're Still in Status Meetings

You bought the PM tool to eliminate the status meeting. The status meeting is still on the calendar.

Here's why, and what's actually different about teams that cancelled it.

---

## The failure mode nobody names

Most PM tools are built for engineering teams: story points, sprint boards, velocity charts. When an operations or marketing team inherits one of these tools, someone spends three weeks configuring it to look like a project tracker, the team uses it for two weeks, and then everyone quietly goes back to Slack threads and spreadsheets.

The meeting stays because the tool never actually answered the question the meeting answers: *what's the status of everything we own, right now, without a 45-minute prep session?*

The failure isn't the team. The failure is a tool that requires an engineering degree to set up and a dedicated admin to maintain.

---

## What "the whole team will actually use" requires

The non-negotiables, based on what separates teams that cancelled the status meeting from teams that didn't:

**Real timelines, not just a Kanban board.** Dependencies matter. If task B can't start until task A ships, the tool needs to know that — and surface it automatically when task A slips. A card on a board doesn't do this.

**Setup that doesn't require IT.** If onboarding your team requires a training doc, a kickoff call, and three weeks, adoption dies in week two. The benchmark to beat: a new team member understands the workspace the first time they open it.

**Status that leaders can pull themselves.** Your VP or COO should be able to open a dashboard and answer "what's the status of the Q2 product launch" without scheduling a meeting with you. If they can't, the meeting stays.

---

## The honest setup conversation

One thing we stopped doing at Stackly: promising 5-minute setup. Eight customers told us it was wrong for their context. The honest benchmark for a non-technical team migrating from scattered tools is 90 minutes to have the team running. Starting from scratch in a new function is typically same day.

That's not 5 minutes. But it's also not a 3-week IT project. And it's without a training document.

> *"Had my team running in 90 minutes. No IT, no training doc."*

---

## The thing to check before your next renewal

If you're evaluating PM tools — or wondering whether to renew the one you have — ask one question during the demo: *Can my VP pull project status themselves without asking me first?*

If the answer requires explaining a filter, building a custom report, or sending a Loom, the meeting isn't going anywhere.
