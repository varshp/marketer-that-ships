# LinkedIn Post — Non-Technical Project Owner

---

Eight customers told us our setup time estimate was wrong for their context.

So we stopped saying "5 minutes."

The honest benchmark for a non-technical team migrating from scattered tools: 90 minutes. No IT ticket. No training doc.

That's still not 5 minutes. But it's the number that holds.

---

The deeper issue with most PM tools isn't the setup promise. It's that they're built for engineering teams — and when ops, marketing, or people teams inherit them, someone spends three weeks configuring a sprint board to look like a project tracker.

Then the team uses it for two weeks.

Then everyone goes back to Slack and spreadsheets.

And the status meeting stays on the calendar.

---

What actually cancels the status meeting isn't a better Kanban board.

It's a view your whole team can read without a manual — real timelines, dependencies that update automatically, and a dashboard your VP can open themselves when they want to know the status of the Q2 launch.

---

If you're evaluating PM tools right now — or about to auto-renew one — I'd ask the vendor one question during the demo:

*Can a non-technical team member understand this workspace the first time they open it?*

If the answer involves training docs, the status meeting isn't going anywhere.
