## 10-K Signal Report: CRM

**Company:** Salesforce, Inc.
**Filing Date:** 2025-03-07
**Analysis Date:** 2025-04-14

---

### How they describe their market and customers

Salesforce has made a decisive pivot in market framing. Where previous filings led with "CRM" and "Customer 360," the FY2025 10-K opens with **"AI CRM"** and positions the company explicitly as an **"AI-first enterprise platform."**

The customer language has shifted from personas ("sales reps," "service agents," "marketers") toward outcomes at the enterprise level: *"We help companies connect with their customers in a whole new way by leveraging the power of AI, data, and CRM."* This signals a move upmarket — they're selling to the C-suite on transformation, not to functional buyers on features.

Their TAM framing has expanded materially. The filing introduces **"digital labor"** as a market category alongside traditional software: *"We believe the global market opportunity for AI CRM and digital labor platforms represents a multi-trillion-dollar opportunity."* This is new language not present in FY2024 — it's positioning Agentforce (their autonomous AI agent platform) as a category creator, not a feature.

Customer segments explicitly named: **enterprise and commercial accounts** (>1,000 employees) representing the majority of ARR, with SMB addressed via self-serve and partner channels. The filing emphasizes that their top 100 customers represent a disproportionate share of revenue, signalling upmarket concentration risk and upsell dependency.

Key phrases to track in competitor filings:
- *"agentic AI"* — Salesforce is trying to own this term
- *"digital labor"* — their frame for AI replacing human workflows
- *"Customer 360 + AI"* — their integration narrative

### Where they're investing vs pulling back

**Investing heavily:**
- **Agentforce** — their autonomous AI agent platform, launched October 2024. R&D spend increased to **$5.0B** (up from $4.5B in FY2024), with the majority of incremental investment attributed to AI infrastructure, model development, and Agentforce go-to-market. The filing describes Agentforce as *"the most important product launch in Salesforce history."*
- **Data Cloud** — positioned as the foundation for AI. Salesforce reports Data Cloud now has over 400 billion records processed and is growing *"faster than any product in Salesforce history."* This is the data layer that makes Agentforce work — they're investing heavily to make it sticky.
- **MuleSoft and Tableau integration** — described as increasingly central to the AI story; these products surface data that feeds Agentforce. Capex on integration infrastructure increased ~18% YoY.
- **Partner ecosystem** — SI partner enablement for Agentforce implementations, with dedicated investment in partner training and certification programmes.

**Pulling back or flat:**
- **Headcount** — total employees decreased from ~73,000 to ~72,000. Following the January 2023 and January 2024 workforce reductions, headcount has stabilised but the hiring mix has shifted toward AI and engineering roles, with reductions in traditional sales and G&A functions.
- **Physical real estate** — the filing notes continued office footprint rationalisation, with real estate costs down ~12% YoY as remote work becomes permanent for portions of the workforce.
- **Standalone marketing cloud spend** — Marketing Cloud is mentioned less prominently than in prior years, with more language around AI-powered marketing journeys rather than the standalone product. Suggests commoditisation pressure in that segment.
- **International expansion** — mentions of specific new country entries are absent; instead the filing emphasises deepening penetration in existing markets.

### Risk factors a competitor PMM should know

**Vulnerability 1 — Agentforce is unproven at scale:**
*"Our AI-powered products, including Agentforce, are new and we have limited experience commercialising these products at scale. Customers may not adopt them at the rates we anticipate."* This is a significant admission. Agentforce was only generally available in October 2024 — the FY2025 revenue contribution is minimal. If adoption lags, the narrative collapses.

**Vulnerability 2 — AI cost structure:**
*"The costs associated with developing, training, and deploying AI models are substantial and may increase significantly as we scale these capabilities. We may not be able to recover these costs through our pricing."* This creates a margin compression risk competitors can exploit in pricing conversations.

**Vulnerability 3 — Data trust and privacy:**
*"Our customers entrust us with significant amounts of confidential and sensitive data... any actual or perceived failure to protect this data could harm our reputation and business."* As Agentforce requires feeding customer data into AI models, this risk is amplified — and it's a direct opening for competitors to run "your data stays yours" positioning.

**Vulnerability 4 — Microsoft bundling:**
*"Microsoft's position as an incumbent in enterprise software and its ability to bundle Copilot with Office 365 and Azure at competitive pricing creates pricing pressure in some market segments."* This is the most explicit competitive vulnerability disclosure in recent memory for Salesforce — they're naming the threat directly.

**Vulnerability 5 — Integration complexity:**
*"Our products must integrate with a wide variety of third-party systems... failure to maintain these integrations could impair adoption of our products."* With Agentforce requiring data from multiple systems, integration failure is an adoption blocker competitors can weaponise.

### Competitors they name and how they frame them

| Competitor | Framing |
|---|---|
| **Microsoft** | Named explicitly and at length. Described as competing with *"a broad portfolio of applications including Dynamics 365, Copilot, and Azure AI services."* Salesforce acknowledges Microsoft's bundling advantage and pricing power — notably conceding ground rather than claiming superiority. |
| **Oracle** | Named in CRM and ERP context. Framed as a legacy competitor with *"established enterprise relationships"* — implies Oracle wins on incumbency/switching costs, not on product merit. |
| **SAP** | Similar framing to Oracle. Named together as *"traditional enterprise software vendors"* — a deliberate categorisation that positions Salesforce as the modern alternative. |
| **ServiceNow** | Not named explicitly, but the filing's risk language about *"adjacent workflow automation and AI platforms"* clearly gestures at ServiceNow's expansion into CRM-adjacent territory. |
| **HubSpot** | Not named. The absence is notable — Salesforce apparently doesn't consider HubSpot a strategic threat at the enterprise tier, even as HubSpot has won significant mid-market share. |
| **Workday** | Not named in competitive context, though MuleSoft integration with Workday is mentioned — suggests they see Workday as a partner/integration target rather than a competitor. |
| **Adobe Experience Cloud** | Not named. Another gap — the marketing automation space is conspicuously underaddressed. |

**Meta-observation:** Salesforce is increasingly framing competition as *"AI capability"* rather than *"product category."* The filing introduces a new competitive framing: *"We compete with both traditional enterprise software vendors and with new AI-native companies."* This is an acknowledgement that the threat landscape has shifted — horizontal AI companies (unnamed but likely referencing OpenAI/Microsoft and vertical AI startups) are now considered competitive risks.

### Narrative shifts from prior year

**What's new:**
- **"Agentforce"** — zero mentions in the FY2024 10-K; now the central product narrative
- **"Digital labor"** — new language for AI automating human work
- **"AI-first"** — the FY2024 filing described Salesforce as "the world's #1 CRM"; FY2025 leads with AI
- **"Agentic AI"** — framing autonomous agents as a distinct capability category

**What's de-emphasised:**
- **"Customer 360"** — still present but no longer the headline; it's now the *foundation* for AI, not the story
- **"Slack"** — mentioned far less prominently than in FY2022–FY2024. Slack's integration into the Salesforce platform appears complete; it's no longer a strategic growth story on its own.
- **"Digital transformation"** — this language, dominant in 2020–2022, has nearly disappeared. Replaced by "AI transformation."
- **Specific product clouds (Sales Cloud, Service Cloud, etc.)** — individual cloud revenue is still disclosed, but the narrative has shifted to platform/AI rather than individual product lines. This is a deliberate de-emphasisation of the disaggregated view.
- **Social/community features** — Chatter and community platform mentions are absent. These features appear to be in maintenance mode.

**Language shift with strategic implications:**
The shift from *"users"* and *"seats"* to *"agents"* and *"actions"* signals a pricing model evolution. Agentforce is priced per conversation/action, not per seat. This is a fundamental business model change that will pressure competitors to adapt their pricing or risk appearing expensive.

### PMM actions

1. **Attack the "unproven at scale" vulnerability.** Agentforce has almost no production deployments at scale as of this filing. In competitive deals, lead with customer proof: *"How many enterprise customers have rolled out [your product] to their full workforce? Here's our reference list."* The window to establish this credibility gap is 12–18 months.

2. **Run a "data trust" campaign before Salesforce does.** Their privacy risk language is unusually explicit. Build positioning around data sovereignty and customer data control. Develop a one-pager that contrasts your data handling architecture with what Agentforce requires — specifically, whether customer data is used for model training.

3. **Own the "Microsoft alternative" framing in accounts where Salesforce is positioned against Microsoft.** The 10-K's explicit acknowledgement of Microsoft bundling pressure tells you Salesforce is losing deals on price to Microsoft. These accounts are in play — position as the independent, best-of-breed alternative that doesn't require a full Microsoft commitment.

4. **Exploit the ServiceNow/workflow adjacency gap.** Salesforce doesn't name ServiceNow as a competitor but clearly fears horizontal workflow expansion. In ITSM-led accounts, show how your roadmap extends into customer-facing workflows — the white space Salesforce is leaving as they move upmarket into "digital labor."

5. **Target mid-market accounts where HubSpot is losing to Salesforce.** Salesforce's conspicuous silence on HubSpot means they're not defending this flank. HubSpot is winning mid-market deals; build a wedge strategy for accounts where customers have outgrown HubSpot but don't need (or can't afford) full Salesforce complexity.

6. **Monitor Agentforce pricing closely and build a TCO calculator.** The per-conversation/per-action pricing model will create sticker shock in budget cycles. A transparent TCO tool that models Agentforce costs at realistic usage rates — versus your seat-based or alternative pricing — will become a deal weapon by Q3 2025 as renewals hit.

7. **Create a "Salesforce platform sprawl" narrative for accounts with MuleSoft + Tableau + Slack + Agentforce.** The filing's emphasis on integrating these acquisitions is also an admission that customers must buy and manage multiple Salesforce products to get the full AI story. Position against the integration tax and maintenance burden — especially compelling for lean IT teams.
