## Competitor: Cortex GTM

### Core pitch
Cortex positions itself as the enterprise-grade AI command centre for revenue teams — a layer that sits above existing CRM and MAP systems, providing cross-tool orchestration and boardroom-ready forecasting.

### Who they're targeting
- **CROs and CFOs at Series C+ companies** who need a single reliable number for board reporting and investor updates.
- **Enterprise RevOps teams** managing complex, multi-product, multi-region sales motions with 10+ integrated tools.
- **Companies post-acquisition** struggling to unify two or more legacy CRM environments into a coherent pipeline view.

### Top 3 positioning bets
1. **AI forecasting with explainability built in.** Rather than a black-box score, Cortex shows exactly which deal-level signals drove each forecast adjustment — giving CFOs and boards the audit trail they need to trust the number.
2. **"Revenue fabric" architecture — connect without ripping and replacing.** Cortex builds connectors for 120+ GTM tools and promises a unified data layer that doesn't require migrating off Salesforce, HubSpot, or Marketo. Reduces political friction of major platform changes.
3. **Dedicated revenue architect on every enterprise contract.** Cortex includes a named human expert who helps configure models, interpret signals, and present results to leadership. This human-in-the-loop premium positions them against purely self-serve AI tools.

### What they're not saying
- Pricing starts at $120,000 ARR, effectively pricing out companies below $20M in revenue.
- The "revenue architect" program has a 6-month wait list; new customers often spend the first quarter without their assigned expert.
- Multi-CRM unification projects routinely run 4–6 months; their "fast-start" guarantee applies only to single-CRM environments.

### Exact customer language
> "For the first time our CFO trusts the forecast number. She can see why it changed week over week, not just that it changed."

> "We had Salesforce and a legacy Dynamics instance from the acquisition. Getting them into one view was the only thing that mattered, and Cortex solved it."
