## PERSONA 1: PMO Lead / Director of Operations at a 50–500 Person Company

### EVIDENCE BASE
- **stackly-positioning-brief.md**: Primary audience named as "PMO leads, ops directors" at "50–500 person companies"; pain points center on "status meetings, spreadsheet budget tracking, and tool sprawl"
- **stackly-content-brief.md**: Hero message addresses "every project, every budget, every dependency in one view"; customer quote "I opened Stackly on Monday morning and saw every project my team owned in one view. I hadn't had that in three years."
- **stackly-positioning-brief.md**: Competitive framing positions against Monday and Asana at "mid-market price point"

### WHO THEY ARE
- **Title/seniority**: Director of Operations, PMO Lead, Head of Project Management (evidence: positioning-brief PRIMARY_AUDIENCE)
- **Company size**: 50–500 employees, typically managing 15–30 concurrent projects across departments (evidence: content-brief AUDIENCE)
- **Day to day**: Running a weekly status meeting that consumes 2+ hours and produces a slide deck nobody reads. Maintaining a project tracker in spreadsheets alongside whatever PM tool the team actually uses. Fielding "what's the status of X?" questions on Slack. Reporting up to leadership on cross-functional initiatives with no single source of truth.
- **What they read/care about**: PM certifications (PMP, PRINCE2), ops leader newsletters, G2 reviews of PM tools, ROI comparisons vs. existing stack

### WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO DO
- **Primary JTBD**: Replace the weekly status meeting with a live view that answers "what's the status of every project" without a meeting (evidence: quote "I opened Stackly on Monday morning...")
- **Secondary JTBD**: Track budget burn against the project plan without a separate spreadsheet; give leadership a real-time dashboard (evidence: positioning-brief "budget-to-task linking is a genuine differentiator")
- **Success**: No more status meetings; leadership can self-serve project health; zero budget surprises

### WHY THEY BUY
- **Trigger**: A project overrun that could have been caught earlier, or a new leadership mandate to consolidate PM tools before renewal season (evidence: positioning-brief competitive window framing)
- **Moving away from**: "Spreadsheet budget tracking" and "tool sprawl" — typically Asana or Monday for tasks + Excel for budgets + Confluence for docs (evidence: content-brief WHAT_TO_AVOID)
- **Moving toward**: "Single pane" consolidated view — every project, every dependency, every dollar (evidence: content-brief HERO_MESSAGE)
- **Internal justification**: Eliminated status meetings (time savings), reduced tool spend, budget overrun prevention (evidence: positioning-brief H1 proof points)

### WHY THEY DON'T BUY
- **Top objection**: Setup time — "5-minute setup" claim is called out as misleading by 8 reviewers; real migration from existing tools takes days, not minutes (evidence: positioning-brief H1_STOP_SAYING)
- **Stall**: Integration reliability — does Stackly pull live data from Jira, GitHub, Slack, or is it another tool to manually update? (evidence: content-brief WHAT_TO_AVOID integration breadth caveat)
- **Who's blocking**: IT/security team on data governance; finance team who owns the budget spreadsheet and is skeptical of PM-tool budget tracking accuracy

### EXACT LANGUAGE THEY USE
- *"I hadn't had that in three years"* (positioning-brief proof point)
- *"The dependency logic is smarter than anything in Monday or Asana at this price point"* (positioning-brief proof point)
- *"Status meetings that exist only to answer questions a dashboard should answer"* (inferred from content-brief positioning)
- *"Budget surprises at month-end"* (inferred from budget-to-task framing)

### WHERE TO REACH THEM
- G2 reviews and vendor comparisons (evidence: positioning-brief H1 proof points cite G2-style review language)
- PMO and ops leader communities (inferred)
- LinkedIn content around project management, consolidation (inferred)

### HORIZON RELEVANCE
**H1** — This is the primary buy today. The H1 claim ("see everything in one view") maps directly to their weekly pain. AI confidence scoring (H2) is a future upsell once they're in the product.

## PERSONA 2: Non-Technical Project Owner / Business Operations Manager

### EVIDENCE BASE
- **stackly-content-brief.md**: "Built for non-technical operations leads who need power without code — not another engineering-first tool"
- **stackly-positioning-brief.md**: H3_FALLBACK explicitly names "ops teams actually adopt — fast setup, real timelines, no engineering degree required"
- **stackly-content-brief.md**: Proof point "Had my team running in 90 minutes. No IT, no training doc."

### WHO THEY ARE
- **Title/seniority**: Business Operations Manager, Marketing Ops Lead, People Ops Manager, Executive Assistant running cross-functional projects (evidence: content-brief positioning)
- **Company size**: 20–200 employees, often a single-person "PM team" or the de facto project coordinator who doesn't hold a formal PM title (evidence: "non-technical operations leads")
- **Day to day**: Owns a handful of cross-functional projects (product launches, hiring campaigns, onboarding programs) using whatever tool people will actually use. Spends more time chasing updates than doing the work. Reports into a VP or COO who wants regular updates without a meeting.
- **What they read/care about**: Productivity blogs (Notion, Linear, Asana tips), "no-code" tool roundups, templates, ease-of-setup content

### WHAT THEY ARE TRYING TO DO
- **Primary JTBD**: Get cross-functional teams aligned on one tool without an IT project or training program (evidence: "No IT, no training doc" proof point)
- **Secondary JTBD**: Show a VP or COO project status on demand without creating a slide deck (evidence: content-brief consolidated visibility message)
- **Success**: The whole team actually uses it; status can be pulled in 30 seconds; setup didn't require a consultant

### WHY THEY BUY
- **Trigger**: A missed deadline or dropped handoff that was blamed on "we didn't have visibility" — or a new COO/VP who asks "what tool do we use to track projects?" (inferred from positioning)
- **Moving away from**: Spreadsheets, Trello, or an overengineered Notion workspace that nobody maintains (evidence: positioning-brief "spending more time organizing your system than actually using it" — Notion competitive frame)
- **Moving toward**: Something that works out of the box with real timelines and dependencies, not just a Kanban board (evidence: H3_FALLBACK "real timelines, no engineering degree required")
- **Internal justification**: Time saved on chasing updates; VP/COO self-serve visibility (inferred)

### WHY THEY DON'T BUY
- **Top objection**: "This will take weeks to set up and nobody will use it" — the 5-minute setup claim triggers skepticism because they've been burned before (evidence: positioning-brief "8 reviews specifically call this out as misleading")
- **Stall**: Fear of adoption failure — they've bought PM tools before that the team ignored after week two (inferred from positioning)
- **Who's blocking**: The team itself, who have tool fatigue; the one engineer who says "just use Linear"

### EXACT LANGUAGE THEY USE
- *"Had my team running in 90 minutes. No IT, no training doc."* (content-brief proof point)
- *"No engineering degree required"* (positioning-brief H3_FALLBACK)
- *"Real timelines, not just a Kanban board"* (inferred from H3 framing)
- *"Something the whole team will actually use"* (inferred from adoption-first framing)

### WHERE TO REACH THEM
- Product Hunt, G2, and template directories (inferred from non-technical buyer profile)
- LinkedIn ads targeting "operations manager" + "project management" (inferred)
- YouTube/blog tutorials for their existing tools (inferred)

### HORIZON RELEVANCE
**H1/H3** — H1 for the visibility pitch; H3 fallback positioning ("actually adopt — fast setup") speaks directly to this persona's core fear. AI features are irrelevant until they trust the core product.

## SEGMENT PRIORITY RECOMMENDATION

**Prioritize Persona 1 (PMO Lead / Director of Operations).**

Evidence from source materials:

1. **Higher contract value**: Persona 1 is evaluating at the organizational level, not the team level — a PMO lead win is a company-wide deployment, not a 5-person team trial.
2. **Budget-to-task linking is the differentiator**: The strongest competitive wedge (budget visibility) is most relevant to Persona 1, who owns both the project plan and the budget conversation with finance.
3. **Competitive window on dependency logic**: "The dependency logic is smarter than anything in Monday or Asana at this price point" (positioning-brief) speaks directly to the PMO lead's evaluation criteria, not the non-technical buyer's.
4. **AI confidence scoring upsell path**: When H2 AI confidence scoring ships, Persona 1 is the natural expansion buyer. Persona 2 is unlikely to care about AI accuracy scoring.

Persona 2 is a volume play for PLG/self-serve; Persona 1 is the strategic enterprise wedge.
